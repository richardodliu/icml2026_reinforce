\subsection{General RLHF}
First, we evaluate \ours in its $k=1$ configuration, focusing on general-domain RLHF, where efficiency and prompt diversity are key.

\paragraph{Experimental Setup}
We use an instruction-following policy model (Llama-3-8B-SFT) and refine it using a Bradley-Terry reward model \citep{bai2022training} trained on $\sim$700K human preference pairs. The policy is trained on 20,000 diverse prompts. We compare \ours ($k=1$) with other critic-free methods: GRPO ($k=4$), RLOO ($k=4$), and ReMax ($k=1$ + 1).

\paragraph{Experimental Results}
As shown in \Cref{tab:general_exp}, the single-sample \textbf{\ours ($k=1$)} achieves a score of 46.7, close to the group-sampling GRPO (46.8). However, it produces shorter responses (832 tokens vs. 860), yielding a higher score/length ratio (0.0561). These measurements describe evaluation performance and generated response length for the single-response and group-sampling configurations.

\paragraph{Results Analysis}
\Cref{fig:orm_reward_kl} shows the training dynamics. GRPO's reward and KL divergence rise rapidly, while \ours exhibits a more gradual reward increase and smaller divergence. These curves describe the reward--KL trade-offs of the compared training configurations. Response length can also influence preference-based evaluation~\citep{dubois2024length}. We report evaluation scores alongside response lengths to make both aspects of the comparison explicit.

\subsection{Reasoning Experiments}
Next, we evaluate \ours (group-sample, $k>1$) in its target domain: complex reasoning, where group sampling is standard, and reward signals are often sparse (e.g., 0/1). We compare it directly with GRPO ($k > 1$).

\subsubsection{Long Reasoning Task}
We use rule-based rewards (RLVR) in mathematical reasoning settings \citep{guo2025deepseek,seed2025seed1}.

\paragraph{Analysis on Small-Scale Datasets}
To test for overfitting, we trained on only 30 questions from AIME-24 and evaluated on AIME-25. As shown in \Cref{tab:small_exp}, GRPO (local norm) achieves a near-perfect 95.0\% on the training set but scores only 0.0 Pass@1 and 0.4 Pass@16 on the test set, showing a large train--test performance gap. In contrast, \ours with global norm, while scoring lower on the training set (71.0), shows higher held-out scores (2.5 Pass@1, 40.0 Pass@16).

\paragraph{Logical Reasoning (K\&K Puzzles)}
We also tested on the Knights and Knaves (K\&K) puzzles \citep{xie2025logic, xie2024memorization}, where difficulty increases with the number of characters in each puzzle. \Cref{fig:logic_score} shows that while GRPO is competitive on easy tasks (2-3 people), it has lower accuracy on the harder eight-character puzzles. \ours outperforms GRPO on the evaluated tasks with four or more characters and achieves a higher average score (62.1 vs. 55.7).

\subsection{Multi-step Reinforcement Learning}
Finally, to test performance in a complex, multi-step environment, we use the zero-shot agent setup from \cite{mai2025agent}, in which a Qwen 2.5 Base 7B model must learn to use Python tools to solve mathematical problems. We evaluate \varn with group sampling ($k > 1$) and group-mean reward centering in this setting (see \Cref{appendix:best}).

\paragraph{Experimental Setup}
We adhere to the training and evaluation protocols of the ZeroTIR environment \cite{mai2025agent}. The backbone model is Qwen 2.5 Base 7B, trained using OpenRLHF \cite{hu2024openrlhf} with datasets from ORZ \cite{hu2025open} and DAPO \cite{yu2025dapo}. We assess performance on benchmarks including AIME 2024 \cite{aime2024card}, AIME-2025 \cite{aime2025card}, HMMT FEB-2024/2025, and CMIMC, using the \texttt{average@32} metric.

\paragraph{Experimental Result}
As shown in \Cref{tab:performance_comparison_main}, \varn achieves the highest average accuracy (24.10) across all benchmarks. It outperforms both GRPO (22.58) and the full-critic PPO (21.85). The improvements in mean accuracy are 1.52 and 2.25 percentage points, respectively. The baseline variant leads on four of the five benchmarks, while GRPO leads on AIME-24. These results evaluate the combined use of reward centering, global normalization, and separate KL regularization.
