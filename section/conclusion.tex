\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We present \ours, a critic-free framework that combines PPO clipping with global advantage normalization. The framework separates the choice of reward baseline from the scale used across prompts. Our scalar analysis characterizes finite-sample self-normalization and its convergence to population standardization under independent Gaussian sampling with a vanishing numerical stabilizer.

The two variants implement this framework for different sampling configurations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \ours uses globally normalized returns with a KL penalty incorporated into the reward. The $k=1$ configuration provides one response per prompt, while $k>1$ supports multiple sampled responses in reasoning tasks.
    \item \varn combines group-mean reward centering, global normalization, and a separate $k_2$ KL surrogate. It uses within-prompt reward comparisons while sharing normalization statistics across the rollout batch.
\end{enumerate}

Experiments cover preference alignment, mathematical and logical reasoning, and multi-step tool use. The single-response variant scores 46.7 on Chat-Arena-Hard, compared with 46.8 for GRPO, with shorter mean responses. The reasoning experiments show higher held-out scores than GRPO across the reported AIME and AMC comparisons. In tool use, the baseline variant achieves the highest mean accuracy across five benchmarks, exceeding GRPO and PPO by 1.52 and 2.25 percentage points. Together, these results support global advantage normalization as a practical component of critic-free policy optimization across the evaluated tasks.
